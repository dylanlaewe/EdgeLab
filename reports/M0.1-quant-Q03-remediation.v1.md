## Root cause and correction

`verify_authority_commitment` already called the common record validator, but
`committed_at` was absent from Data's shared `COMPLETED_TIMES` registry. JSON Schema
therefore enforced timestamp syntax while the self-digest enforced only integrity
of the attacker-selected value; neither rejected a correctly rehashed year-2999
commitment.

The correction adds `committed_at` to the shared completed-fact clock registry.
Consequently a commitment must carry a valid offset-aware timestamp no later than
the verifier-supplied evaluation time. The check runs through the existing
`validate_record` path before local binding verification. Recomputing
`commitment_sha256` cannot turn a future completed fact into a plausible one.

Owner coverage verifies:

- the valid completed timestamp returned by the synthetic authority is accepted;
- malformed timestamp syntax is rejected by schema validation;
- a non-future timestamp altered without rehash is rejected by the commitment
  digest;
- a future timestamp is rejected; and
- a future timestamp with a correctly recomputed self-digest still rejects as
  `Future completed fact: committed_at`.

## Quant/Risk boundary

authority ID/generation, commitment identity/time, assessment, current state,
experiment, exact claim references, limitations and self-digest. Timestamp
plausibility now also rejects a claimed completed fact later than verifier `now`.

global uniqueness, key custody, adapter honesty, controller authentication,
durable storage or true external atomicity. A self-consistent forged record and
duplicate verification remain possible without an authenticated persistent
authority/Risk registry. Those controls remain Risk/external-trust responsibilities
and were not implemented here.
