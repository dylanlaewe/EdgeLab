# External authority provisioning plan for STOP-M0-001

## Verdict

The clearance prerequisite requires **C + D + a small Risk-owned verification integration**: externally controlled signing/identity material and an external identity or key-management service. Repository-only changes cannot create a legitimate trust root.

## Minimum trust root

The human project owner may serve as the initial root administrator, but not as every clearance actor. The owner must create an external, independently controlled authority service with a hardware- or cloud-KMS-protected root signing key. The root public-key fingerprint must be supplied to the EdgeLab verifier from a configuration channel outside the candidate repository. The root private key must never be exposed to Codex, Git, the repository checkout, environment logs, or agent prompts.

The root signs a versioned authority policy that contains the schema-required policy ID, generation, controller ID, `VERIFIED_EXTERNAL` authentication declaration, external source locator, validity interval and canonical actor mapping. The policy must also name a revocation/update mechanism and externally authenticated delegation identities. A repository copy may be a cached, signed evidence artifact only; the verifier must fetch or receive the signed policy from the configured external source and validate it against the out-of-band root fingerprint.

## Required clearance actors

The clearance policy must map each role to a distinct canonical external actor with `STOP_CLEAR` scope:

| Required role | Required actor property |
|---|---|
| 01 Data | Distinct from every remediation author it reviews; validates Data/H04/H06/M01/M02 evidence. |
| 03 Research | Distinct; validates Research/R01/H05 evidence. |
| 04 Quant | Distinct; validates Quant/Q01–Q03 evidence. |
| 05 Skeptic | Distinct; acts as original STOP issuer or documented independent substitute. |
| 06 Risk | Distinct; required because STOP scope includes capital controls. |

The policy administrator/root is not itself an approval actor unless it has a separately authenticated canonical identity and no conflict. Actor IDs, aliases, authority IDs and authority generations must be one-to-one. No actor may approve its own remediation, an actor with a declared conflict, a revoked actor, or a stale policy generation. Codex chat labels, role files, Git authorship and repository JSON do not establish identity or independence.

For future autonomy, these actors may be externally authenticated verification services with separate workload identities, key access policies and independently validated evidence inputs. They may not be five aliases for one Codex process or one unrestricted credential. If such separation cannot be operated, human independent reviewers must remain in the loop and clearance stays blocked until they do.

## Required evidence and typed records

Before any clearance attempt, create a content-hashed remediation-evidence manifest listing the exact final handoffs, reviews, current test reports, preservation inventories, current project/portfolio bytes and revalidation output. Each approval attestation must bind:

- `subject_type: STOP_RESOLUTION`, exact event ID and event SHA-256;
- the evidence-manifest SHA-256;
- exact policy ID/generation, actor alias, role, `STOP_CLEAR` scope, issue/expiry times and conflict disclosures;
- an external signature or service-verifiable credential binding the full attestation bytes.

The candidate `stop-resolution-event` must bind the preserved `STOP-M0-001.v1` ID/version/canonical digest, acknowledgement, the exact remediation-evidence path/digest set, `descendants_revalidated: true`, all required roles, capital implication, canonical remediation-author actor IDs and its own digest. It must be accompanied by a verifier-selected current OPEN STOP head. A future, stale, altered, incomplete or replayed event denies.

`remediation_author_actor_ids` must include the canonical actors that authored the Data, Research, Quant and Risk remediation evidence. Every clearer must be distinct from, and conflict-free with, those authors. The policy must explicitly require all five roles above for this global M0 STOP; a lesser test-fixture policy is not sufficient for this clearance.

## Replay, generation and impersonation controls

The external service must issue unique attestation IDs and event IDs, retain an append-only consumed-clearance ledger, and reject a repeated or superseded event, policy-generation downgrade, revoked credential, stale policy or expired attestation. EdgeLab's local replay store remains only a secondary local check. The external verifier must compare the policy generation, STOP head and evidence-manifest digest immediately before acceptance.

Future Codex agents cannot impersonate the authority because they do not possess the external signing credential or workload identity, cannot alter the out-of-band root fingerprint, and cannot cause the external verifier to sign without independently checking the bound evidence. A missing, invalid, unavailable, stale, revoked or unverifiable authority artifact must fail closed; it does not permit a fallback repository policy.

## Storage and Git rules

Safe to commit: schemas, verifier code, public-key fingerprints only when independently pinned outside Git, signed policy/attestation/resolution evidence copies, public certificates, opaque external record IDs, hashes, revocation metadata and `.env.example` variable names.

Never commit: private keys, KMS credentials, OAuth client secrets/tokens, service-account JSON, recovery codes, unredacted external identity records, or replay-store secrets. Store private material in a human-controlled HSM/KMS or secret manager. Local development references may be in an ignored `.env` file with restrictive permissions; agents receive only short-lived, least-privilege verifier credentials if automation is later approved.

## Required engineering before use

Current Risk code checks only a caller-supplied policy object and source string; it does not verify an external signature or retrieve a policy independently. Before provisioning can support clearance, Risk must implement and Skeptic independently review: signed policy and attestation fields, root-fingerprint pinning outside the repository, a verifier-controlled policy provider, signature/credential verification, external replay/current-generation lookup, and fail-closed outage behavior. Do not generate keys or configure services until the human owner selects the external trust provider and authorizes that engineering scope.

## Sequence

1. Human owner selects and controls the external KMS/identity provider and root public-key pin channel.
2. Human creates the root key and external policy source; private material remains external.
3. Director releases a bounded Risk integration assignment; Risk implements the verifier boundary and tests it.
4. Skeptic independently reviews the integration and its outage/replay/revocation behavior.
5. External policy delegates five distinct clearance actors or services and publishes the signed current generation.
6. Director assembles a non-authorizing candidate evidence manifest and resolution event from frozen current evidence.
7. Each external domain actor independently verifies its scope and signs a typed approval attestation.
8. A verifier validates signatures, policy generation, conflicts, STOP head, evidence hashes, descendants and replay state.
9. Only then may Director validate the typed clearance and publish a superseding STOP record for a new final M0 gate.

This plan does not create authority material, clear STOP-M0-001, begin M1, activate Scout or unlock capital.
